1. History of Present Illness (HPI)
Patient is a 58-year-old female with a past medical history significant for hypertension and polycystic kidney disease who presents via Emergency Medical Services with a sudden onset severe thunderclap headache that began approximately 90 minutes prior to arrival. Patient describes the pain as reaching 10/10 intensity within seconds while sitting at home watching television. She reports associated bilateral photophobia, nausea, one episode of non-bilious emesis, and stiffness in her posterior neck. She denies preceding trauma, fever, chills, visual field cuts, focal limb weakness, or loss of consciousness.
2. Review of Systems (ROS)
Constitutional: Positive for severe distress secondary to pain. Denies fever or chills. Neurological: Positive for acute thunderclap headache, photophobia, and nuchal rigidity. Denies numbness, tingling, weakness, or seizure activity. Gastrointestinal: Positive for nausea and one episode of emesis. Cardiovascular: Denies chest pain, palpitations, or orthopnea. Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath, cough, or dyspnea. All other systems reviewed and are negative.
3. Physical Examination
Vitals: BP 188/104, HR 88, RR 16, SpO2 98% on room air, Temp 36.8 C. General: Patient is lying supine in hospital bed, wearing sunglasses due to photophobia, in acute discomfort. Head/Neck: Normocephalic, atraumatic. Nuchal rigidity is present with resistance on passive anterior cervical flexion. No carotid bruits. Cardiovascular: Regular rate and rhythm. S1 and S2 present. No murmurs, rubs, or gallops. Pulmonary: Lungs clear to auscultation bilaterally. No wheezes, rales, or rhonchi. Abdomen: Soft, non-tender, non-distended. Normal active bowel sounds. Neurological: Awake, alert, and oriented to person, place, time, and situation. Cranial nerves II through XII intact. Pupils are 3 mm bilaterally and briskly reactive to light. Extraocular movements intact without nystagmus. Motor strength is 5/5 in all four extremities. Pronator drift is absent. Sensation intact to light touch throughout. Normal gait deferred due to acute illness.
4. Emergency Department Course & Medical Decision Making (MDM)
Patient presents with classic symptoms concerning for intracranial hemorrhage, specifically subarachnoid hemorrhage given sudden onset thunderclap headache and known history of polycystic kidney disease (associated with berry aneurysms). STAT non-contrast CT of the head was immediately ordered upon arrival along with complete blood count, comprehensive metabolic panel, and coagulation studies. CT Head demonstrated hyperdensity within the basal cisterns and bilateral Sylvian fissures consistent with acute subarachnoid hemorrhage. Initial systolic blood pressure was elevated at 188 mmHg. Nicardipine IV infusion was initiated at 5 mg/hr and titrated to achieve a strict systolic blood pressure goal below 140 mmHg to minimize risk of re-bleeding. Nimodipine 60 mg orally was administered for vasospasm prophylaxis. Emergent neurosurgery consultation was paged. Dr. Vance evaluated the patient at bedside and recommended admission to the Neurological Intensive Care Unit with emergent cerebral digital subtraction angiography for definitive endovascular coiling or surgical clipping.
5. Assessment & Plan
1. Acute Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (Hunt and Hess Grade 2, Fisher Grade 3) - Continue Nicardipine IV infusion titrated for SBP less than 140. - Maintain Nimodipine 60 mg PO every 4 hours for 21 days. - Admit to Neuro-ICU under Neurosurgery service. - Proceed with STAT cerebral angiography. 2. Hypertensive Emergency / Elevated Intracranial Pressure - Bed rest with head of bed elevated 30 degrees. - Neurological checks every 1 hour. - Full code maintained.
